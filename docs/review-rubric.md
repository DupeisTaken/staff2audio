# Independent acceptance rubric

This rubric evaluates a local application that accepts score screenshots and PDFs, identifies staff systems, notes and key signatures, and plays recognized music using a grand piano sound. The supported recognition target is **clean printed monophonic music**. Acceptance must describe that scope clearly; arbitrary handwritten, polyphonic or orchestral score recognition is a separate capability and is not implied by a high score here.

The independent reviewer owns the assessment. Implementation completion, a passing automated suite, attractive screenshots, or a requested score cannot substitute for observed evidence. Scores remain provisional until the final integrated build is inspected. A review may report a score below the target and specify what remains to be fixed.

## Scoring and evidence rules

- Every dimension below must score **strictly greater than 9.5/10** to meet the requested acceptance threshold. There is no averaging away a weak dimension.
- A 9.6–10 score requires direct evidence for the critical paths, representative positive and negative cases, and no known material defect in the supported scope. 10 means all planned evidence is present and no material gap is known, not that every possible input is proven correct.
- Missing meaningful evidence caps the affected dimension at 9.0. A known wrong supported result that is presented as reliable caps recognition or reliability at 8.0 until repaired. A crash, unusable primary flow, private-data leak, missing runtime asset, or false success claim blocks acceptance regardless of average score.
- Record actual commands, input types, output, and screenshots. Label unrun checks, inferred properties, and residual limits explicitly. Re-run affected checks after meaningful fixes.
- Ground-truth fixtures must be independently specified. Do not accept expected values generated by the same recognition code, filenames that encode answers, input-specific branches, or synthetic fixtures alone as evidence of general recognition.
- Use the supplied private PDF only locally. Do not commit it, its page images, identifying excerpts, or private recognition outputs. Public review artifacts must use synthetic or openly licensed examples.
- Serialize heavy tests, builds, audio processing, and browser verification with the lead agent. Stop only task-owned processes and retain user work.

## Minimum baseline test matrix

### Recognition and score handling

1. Clean treble staff in C major: ascending and descending notes, staff/space positions and ledger notes; exact MIDI and order comparison.
2. Clean bass staff: the same staff positions must produce bass-clef pitches, not treble pitches with a cosmetic label.
3. Key signatures of opposite signs and multiple symbols: distinguish their pre-note position from accidentals; apply the signature to the correct pitch classes and octaves.
4. Explicit sharp, flat and natural, plus repeated pitch in a measure and after the next barline where supported; if the recognizer cannot model persistence, the UI must disclose the limitation.
5. Durations/rests actually claimed by the product; compare beat-based onsets and durations. Whole/half versus filled noteheads, stems, flags/beams and dotted notes require either correct treatment or a visible scope warning.
6. Multiple staff systems with different vertical placement, page scaling/resizing and mild screenshot noise; no duplicate notes from staff-line removal or clef/key components.
7. Empty, staff-only, cropped, low-resolution, rotated and unsupported polyphonic/handwritten inputs; no false high-confidence success.
8. PDF/image decode boundaries, valid multi-page selection, invalid page, no staff and malformed file; inspect response codes and user-facing messages.
9. Independently inspect a representative supplied-PDF page locally. Record only aggregate outcomes in public artifacts. A successful synthetic suite alone cannot close this check.

### Audio and user journeys

1. Verify actual sample decoding and audible playback at low, middle and high pitches. Check sample attribution and bundle integrity.
2. Use a short known melody with a rest and unequal durations. Compare callback sequence/timing and listen for alignment with highlighted notes.
3. Pause and resume during a sustained note; stop and restart; seek forward/back; change selected staff/page while playing; click play repeatedly. No old schedule may remain audible or overwrite the current status.
4. Exercise supported tempo/transposition limits and volume zero/max. End, loop and empty events must terminate or repeat predictably.
5. Download exports if offered. Parse MIDI events/tempo and inspect WAV duration/header/non-silence against an independent expectation.
6. Complete the primary journey on a desktop viewport and a narrow mobile viewport using the actual server. Inspect both available languages, keyboard focus and error recovery.
7. Run with browser network logging: local operation should remain usable without external network requests after local startup.

## Final review record

For each dimension, record: score, observed evidence, known limitations, and required fixes. Include the reviewed commit or exact working-tree state and test environment. State whether the threshold was achieved. Reassess after fixes; do not raise a score merely because the requested target is difficult.
